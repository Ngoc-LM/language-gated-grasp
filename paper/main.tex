\begin{abstract}
Language-driven grasp detection requires placing a grasp on the object part named by a free-form prompt. We steer GR-ConvNet, a lightweight grasp network, with FiLM and a \emph{language gate}, a spatial map of cosine similarities between frozen CLIP patch tokens and the prompt embedding. Trained on 36k Grasp-Anything++ images, the model reaches 68.6\% seen and 57.8\% unseen success (two-seed mean) on the annotated official test split. A gate that cannot read the prompt loses 9.9 unseen points, so about half of the gain over FiLM comes from prompt-dependent gating rather than stronger visual features. Code: \url{https://github.com/Ngoc-LM/language-gated-grasp}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A parallel-jaw grasp is a rotated rectangle $g=(x,y,w,h,\theta)$: centre, opening, jaw size and angle~\cite{jiang2011efficient}. Language-driven grasp detection conditions $g$ on a prompt such as ``grasp the knife by its handle'', so the model must first find \emph{which} part is meant. Grasp-Anything++~\cite{vuong2024language} provides 1M images, over 10M such prompts and a diffusion model, LGD; we instead steer a small single-pass grasp CNN. Our contributions are (i) a \emph{language gate} that re-weights GR-ConvNet~\cite{kumra2020antipodal} features by CLIP~\cite{radford2021learning} patch--text similarity, adding 0.33M parameters (\cref{fig:arch}); (ii) a controlled ablation (text-free gate, second seed) that separates CLIP visual features from prompt-dependent gating; and (iii) a leakage-controlled evaluation with open code.

\section{Related Work}
Generative grasp CNNs~\cite{morrison2018closing,kumra2020antipodal} regress dense grasp maps and decode the best pixel. CLIPort~\cite{shridhar2022cliport} fuses CLIP features into ``what/where'' streams for manipulation. LGD~\cite{vuong2024language} denoises grasps under text guidance. MaskCLIP~\cite{zhou2022extract} localises named objects with dense CLIP features; our gate instead learns lightweight projections that align CLIP patch tokens with the prompt.

\section{Method}
\textbf{Grasp maps.} As in~\cite{kumra2020antipodal}, the network predicts four $224{\times}224$ maps: quality $Q$, $\cos2\Theta$, $\sin2\Theta$ and opening $W$. The angle is doubled because a grasp is unchanged by a $180^\circ$ rotation. Targets fill each ground-truth rectangle shortened to the central third of its opening. The smoothed maps are read at $\arg\max Q$, with $\theta=\tfrac12\operatorname{atan2}(\sin2\Theta,\cos2\Theta)$ and $h=w/2$.

\textbf{FiLM.} The encoder maps the image to $F\in\mathbb{R}^{128\times56\times56}$, followed by five residual blocks and a decoder. With $t$ the normalised CLIP text embedding, each residual block $k$ is followed by
\begin{equation}
F \leftarrow \big(1+\Delta\gamma_k(t)\big)\odot F + \beta_k(t),
\end{equation}
where $\Delta\gamma_k,\beta_k\in\mathbb{R}^{128}$ come from a zero-initialised linear layer, so each FiLM layer is the identity at initialisation.

\textbf{Language gate (proposed).} FiLM applies the same channel modulation at every pixel, so it can favour one object among several only indirectly. We therefore compare the $14{\times}14$ post-LayerNorm patch tokens $P$ of a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 with the prompt and gate the encoder output:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
S_{ij}=\cos(W_p\,p_{ij},\,W_t\,t),\\
A=\mathrm{Up}_{56}\big(\sigma(\tau S)\big),\qquad F'=F\odot(1+A),
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with learned $W_p\in\mathbb{R}^{256\times768}$, $W_t\in\mathbb{R}^{256\times512}$ and temperature $\tau$ (initialised to 10). The residual form $1{+}A$ only amplifies prompt-matching regions. The \textbf{C0 control} replaces $W_t t$ with a learned vector $q$: its gate sees the same CLIP features but not the prompt (FiLM still receives $t$). \textbf{Loss:} Smooth-$\ell_1$ summed over the four maps. Variant D adds $0.5\,\mathrm{BCE}(\tau S, M)$, $M$ the pooled ground-truth $Q$ mask.

\section{Experiments}
\textbf{Protocol.} We train on 35,964 Grasp-Anything++ scenes (183,559 prompt--grasp pairs) and validate on 734. We test on the official LGD split (the 70\% most frequent LVIS categories are \emph{seen}), restricted to its annotated pairs: 1,376 seen and 601 unseen (727 of 803 object keys). Test scenes and training pairs naming unseen test objects are excluded from training. A top-1 grasp is correct if its IoU exceeds 0.25 and its angle error is below $30^\circ$ for some ground-truth grasp of the prompted part. $H$ is the harmonic mean of seen and unseen success. Decoding ground-truth maps scores 97.5\% (the encoding's ceiling). All models use AdamW (lr $10^{-3}$, cosine), batch 32, 6 epochs, no augmentation and one Kaggle T4 (2.5--2.7\,h), with checkpoints selected on 1,000 validation pairs.

\textbf{Results.} \cref{tab:main} is a chain of controlled steps. Model A ignores the prompt, so seen ${\approx}$ unseen. FiLM (B) adds 17.8 points of $H$, and CLIP features behind a text-free gate (C0) add 9.1. Letting the gate read the prompt (C) adds 8.4 more, with a larger gain on unseen (+9.9) than seen (+5.9) objects (\cref{fig:qual}). This gap exceeds C's seed range (3.8) and the 95\% interval of a two-proportion difference ($\pm5.6$). Gate supervision (D) adds 2.2 seen points (within binomial noise) and leaves unseen and $H$ unchanged; we keep C.

\textbf{Cost and limitations.} C also runs a frozen 149M-parameter CLIP; with the prompt embedding cached, a frame takes 12.5\,ms. We use 3.6\% of the images, one seed for most variants and only annotated test pairs, so absolute numbers are not comparable to~\cite{vuong2024language}.

\section{Conclusion}
A frozen-CLIP patch--text gate lifts a lightweight grasp CNN from 27.5 to 62.8 $H$, half of the gain over FiLM coming from prompt-dependent gating.
