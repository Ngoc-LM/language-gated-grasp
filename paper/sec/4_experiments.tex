\section{Experiments}
\textbf{Protocol.} Training uses 35,927 Grasp-Anything++ scenes (183,559 prompt--grasp pairs) and validation 734 scenes. Testing follows the official LGD seen/unseen split, restricted to annotated pairs: 1,376 seen and 601 unseen from 727 of 803 object keys. Test scenes, and training pairs whose object name is an unseen test name, are excluded from training; a word search finds unseen names in 5,332 training pairs, all as parts of other objects, and 40 of the 41 seen names occur in training. A top-1 grasp is correct if its IoU exceeds 0.25 and its angle error is below $30^\circ$ for some ground-truth grasp of the prompted part; $H$ is the harmonic mean of seen and unseen success. All runs use AdamW (lr $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, 500 warm-up steps, cosine), batch 32 and 6 epochs on one T4; checkpoints are selected on 1,000 validation pairs.

\textbf{Results.} FiLM (B) raises $H$ by 19.2 points, CLIP features behind the text-free gate (C0) add 6.6, and letting the gate read the prompt (C) adds 9.4. Seed for seed, C exceeds C0 by 11.6/11.8 unseen and 6.3/4.4 seen points; paired McNemar tests give $p<10^{-3}$ in all four comparisons, and scene-resampled 95\% bootstrap intervals exclude zero. The unseen gain of C0 over B varies between seeds ($+7.8$, $p<10^{-3}$; $+2.8$, $p=0.21$), whereas the C0$\to$C gain is stable. In the 118 multi-object scenes, C solves two prompts on different objects in 55, C0 in 40 (seed 0, \cref{fig:qual}). Prompt-dependent gating thus gives 69\% of the B$\to$C gain on unseen objects, 59\% in $H$ and 38\% on seen ones. D does not differ significantly from C ($p\geq0.06$).

\textbf{Cost and limitations.} Over B, C adds 0.33M trained parameters and the frozen 86M CLIP image encoder (13.0\,ms/frame with a cached prompt). A already exceeds the 18\% unseen reported for GR-ConvNet with CLIP~\cite{vuong2024language}, so LGD's 48/42\% are not compared.
